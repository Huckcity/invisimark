Invisible vs visible watermarks: which do you need?

They solve different problems. A visible watermark discourages and claims ownership. An invisible per-recipient mark tells you who a leaked copy came from. Often you want both.

When a visible watermark is enough

When you only want to say "this is mine" on something you are happy for people to see and share, such as a portfolio, a preview or a social post. It costs nothing and signals ownership immediately.

When you need an invisible one

When you give the same file to specific people and need to know who passed it on: custom work, paid downloads, course material, review copies, pre-release builds and footage under embargo. Because each copy is different, the leak itself carries the evidence.

Using both

Put a visible mark on public previews, and an invisible per-recipient mark in the files you hand over. Tell recipients their copy is individually marked. In most cases that does more to prevent leaks than any technical measure.

What neither does

Neither stops copying, and neither is proof on its own. An invisible mark gives you evidence about whose copy leaked, which you should check against how and when you sent each copy before acting on it.
